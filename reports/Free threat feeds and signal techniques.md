# Fix seven integrations, then mine captures

The largest gains available to the platform do not come from new feeds. They come from three places: repairing existing integrations that are blind or outside their licence, extracting attribution evidence from page captures the platform already stores, and turning email authentication into a first-party sensor. Seven current integrations have a problem found during this research. The most serious is **the Spamhaus DBL enrichment, which returns "clean" for every domain**: it queries through Cloudflare's public resolver, which Spamhaus answers with an error code that the code reads as "not listed". The ransomware leak-site ingest reads one source frozen since June 2025 and another whose terms prohibit commercial use. On the additions side, only a short list of sources is both alive and free for a paid product: CERT Polska, phishunt, Infoblox, Spamhaus ASN-DROP, RansomLook, MISP Galaxy, and the OpenSSF and Datadog malicious-package sets. Most well-known "free" data is non-commercial, including Shodan InternetDB, SANS ISC, Maltrail trails, Malpedia, Ransomware.live, the Spamhaus query services and the Cloudflare Radar API. The best new signal type is hosted DMARC aggregate reporting, which shows per brand which IP addresses send mail spoofing the customer's exact domain. The best technique is a page-content rules engine in the open IOK format plus artifact co-occurrence clustering in SQL. The evidence is uneven: licence and liveness facts were mostly checked against primary pages, while most effectiveness numbers are vendor telemetry and several promising pivots have no published evidence at all.

Three further items need a decision, not a repair. The integrated Hagezi NRD list comes from a repository licensed GPL-3.0 ([LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hagezi/dns-blocklists/main/LICENSE)). Whether copyleft on a data list binds a service that only consumes it server-side is a legal question the research did not resolve. The DMARC parser in `email-security.ts` still reads `pct`, which DMARCbis deprecated. RFC 9989, published in May 2026, replaces RFC 7489, adds the `np`, `psd` and `t` tags, and replaces the Public Suffix List with a DNS tree walk to find the governing record ([Red Sift](https://redsift.com/blog/dmarc-rfc-9989)). The posture scanner should stop scoring `pct` and start checking `np`. Certificate transparency is safe for now: the platform searches crt.sh instead of reading logs directly, so the retirement of Let's Encrypt's RFC 6962 logs does not break it. Those logs went read-only on 30 November 2025 with shutdown planned for 28 February 2026 ([Let's Encrypt](https://letsencrypt.org/2025/08/14/rfc-6962-logs-eol.html)). The platform does remain dependent on one third-party index.

Two changes need no work. ICANN ended the gTLD WHOIS obligation on 28 January 2025, leaving RDAP as the required interface, which the platform already uses ([ICANN](https://www.icann.org/en/contracted-parties/registry-operators/global-amendments/2023-global-amendments)). Google Postmaster Tools v2 no longer exposes domain or IP reputation, so it is not worth pursuing as a customer add-on ([Google](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/16594218)).

Tier A is ordered by how little stands between the platform and the signal. The IOK engine comes first because it needs no new data: its nine fields are server HTML, post-JavaScript DOM, scripts, stylesheets, cookies, headers, requests, title and hostname, which is what a headless capture yields ([IOK reference](https://phish.report/docs/iok-rule-reference)). ASN-DROP is one small daily JSON from a publisher the platform already ingests. RansomLook is a replacement for broken sources, not an expansion.

Among the feeds, phishunt's inputs overlap the integrated set (certificate transparency, Safe Browsing, urlscan, OpenPhish, TweetFeed), so its value is the brand label and hosting enrichment on about 940 active URLs, not novelty ([phishunt feed](https://phishunt.io/feed/)). It is a one-person project, so do not make it a critical dependency. CERT Polska covers a population the current feeds barely touch, but only sites that target Polish users. Its 22 MB JSON should be avoided in favour of the 2.6 MB domain list or the add-and-remove log.

The malicious-package sets open a surface the platform does not watch at all. The existing typosquat matcher can run over package names, and withdrawn false-positive reports are tracked upstream ([OpenSSF](https://redirect.github.com/ossf/malicious-packages)). The actor spine gives the no-AI Attributor a canonical alias table: MISP Galaxy lists 1,118 threat actors and 2,180 ransomware entries ([MISP Galaxy](https://github.com/MISP/misp-galaxy)), and the Microsoft map adds about 184 cross-vendor names ([MicrosoftMapping.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/mstic/master/PublicFeeds/ThreatActorNaming/MicrosoftMapping.json)).

## Email authentication becomes a first-party spoofing sensor

Hosted aggregate reporting is the highest-value new signal type because nothing else supplies it. A domain owner publishes a `rua=` address in its DMARC record, and receiving mail providers send daily XML reports. Each row carries the source IP, message count, disposition, SPF and DKIM results, and the header-from, envelope-from and DKIM signing domains ([RFC 7489 Appendix C](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7489#appendix-C)). To receive reports for a customer's domain, the platform publishes an authorisation record at `<customer-domain>._report._dmarc.<platform-domain>` ([RFC 7489 §7.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7489#section-7.1)). Joined to the existing ASN and cluster tables, rows that fail both aligned checks become a per-brand list of spoofing infrastructure.

The limit is structural. Aggregate reports cover only mail using the customer's exact domain, never lookalike domains, and only receivers that send reports. No source measured what share of spoofed mail they reveal. Forensic (RUF) reports are a poor investment on current knowledge, but that judgement was not confirmed this session. TLS-RPT is a second report stream with an `https:` delivery option a Worker can receive directly ([RFC 8460](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8460)). Its failures are far more often an expired certificate than an attack, so it belongs in operational alerting.

The cheapest posture findings are the ones attackers have already exploited at scale. The SubdoMailing campaign abused about **8,000 domains and 13,000 subdomains to send roughly five million emails a day**. It worked by re-registering expired domains that were still named in a brand's SPF `include:` or pointed to by a subdomain's CNAME ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/domains-once-owned-by-major-firms-help-millions-of-spam-emails-bypass-security/), [Kaspersky](https://www.kaspersky.com/blog/domain-hijacking-subdomailing/51097/)). Flattening each brand's SPF tree and checking whether every target's registered domain still exists yields a high-severity finding with almost no false positives when RDAP confirms the domain is unregistered. BreakSPF supplies a second citable check: **23,916 domains in the Tranco top million** had SPF records that authorised shared cloud, proxy or CDN address pools, and 51.7% of domains authorise more than 65,536 addresses ([NDSS 2024 paper](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2024-113-paper.pdf), [slides](https://ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2024-113-slides.pdf)). SPF evaluation also fails outright past ten DNS-querying mechanisms ([RFC 7208](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7208#section-4.6.4)).

On lookalike domains, MX presence alone is too common to alert on. Two 2026 scans put it at about 16% of 5.99 million lookalikes of the top 1,000 brands and 48% of a 591-domain sample ([WhoisFreaks](https://whoisfreaks.com/resources/case-study/typosquatting-and-the-top-1-000-brands-a-2026-census), [Hard2bit](https://hard2bit.com/en/blog/typosquatting-scan-24-brands-591-domains/)). A 2017 academic study gives the stronger pivot: millions of typosquatting domains pointed their MX at "only a handful of mail servers" ([Szurdi and Christin, IMC 2017](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/nicolasc/publications/Szurdi-IMC17.pdf)). So the platform should alert on the *change* (an MX appearing on a lookalike already matched to a brand), cluster by MX host, and raise severity when the lookalike mirrors the brand's own mail provider, as in documented invoice-fraud cases ([IRONSCALES](https://ironscales.com/threat-intelligence/lookalike-domain-display-name-impersonation-zero-payload-trust-building)). Treat this as a severity raiser, not proof. No study measured how often, or how soon, an MX on a lookalike precedes fraud.

For customer-facing benchmarks, always name the sample, because vendor figures disagree. EasyDMARC found 52.1% of the top 1.8 million domains with a valid DMARC record in early 2026 and 22.9% at quarantine or reject ([EasyDMARC](https://easydmarc.com/blog/dmarc-adoption-report-2026/)). Palisade found 35.6% at `p=reject` among top-100,000 domains that publish a record ([Palisade](https://www.palisade.email/research/state-of-dmarc-2026)). BIMI mark-certificate monitoring is cheap but speculative: mark certificates must be logged publicly before issuance ([BIMI Group](https://bimigroup.org/mva-faqs/)), yet no documented case of fraudulent issuance was found.

## Captured pages already hold the attribution evidence

The platform captures HTML, scripts, headers and SHA-256 hashes for evidence. The research says those same artifacts identify the kit. A study of 1.33 million phishing URLs defined kit families as sets of source-file hashes with Jaccard similarity of at least 0.9. Clustering pages by script hashes alone scored 0.80 to 0.89 against those labels, and clustering by the set of browser APIs called scored 0.97 ([arXiv 2509.13186](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13186v1)). The caveat matters: one mass-deployed Wells Fargo kit was 24% of the labelled sample, and rebalancing it away dropped the best score to 0.69. Any accuracy measured on the platform's own corpus will be flattered by its most common kit.

The practical design is one narrow table of `(capture, kind, value)` rows for script hashes, header fingerprints, cookie names, tracker IDs, nameserver sets and matched rule IDs. A self-join then feeds the existing connected-components pass. This is the researchers' proposal, not a published method. Two published guards shape it. urlscan warns that a file hash shared only because it is a common library over-matches and must be paired with a second indicator ([urlscan](https://urlscan.io/blog/2026/08/04/ProveFinancialPhishingFramework/)). A Keitaro detection framework requires two independent artifact checks before declaring a match ([PhishDestroy](https://phishdestroy.io/keitaro-tds-exposed)). That mirrors the platform's hub-IP and shared-ASN rules.

Which pivots last is the useful finding. Re-tests of earlier investigations found that server response fingerprints (banner and header hashes), nameservers and registration patterns kept finding new infrastructure months later, while favicons, page titles, IPs and CSS hashes went stale ([Validin re-test, via mirror](https://www.hendryadrian.com/?p=78289), [six-month follow-up, via mirror](https://www.hendryadrian.com/?p=101623)). urlscan reports the same for nameservers against IPs and for kit backend cookie names ([urlscan](https://urlscan.io/blog/2026/09/03/FluxPanelandFastFlux/)). So header fingerprints and cookie names should carry the most weight. These claims rest on vendor case studies read through search summaries, not controlled measurement.

TLS fingerprints are a poor fit on two counts. The researchers' assessment, not confirmed this session, is that a Worker's `fetch()` does not expose the server handshake. Separately, the server-side JA4+ fingerprints are licensed against monetisation: only plain JA4 is BSD, and a vendor selling JA4+ in a product "would need to request an OEM license" ([FoxIO](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FoxIO-LLC/ja4/main/README.md)).

Timing argues for speed on a small set of domains. One vendor measured almost 60% of new phishing domains holding a working certificate within two hours ([Zimperium](https://zimperium.com/blog/deep-dive-into-phishing-chronology-threats-and-trends)). A 2021 academic study measured a 24-hour median phishing-domain uptime ([Bijmans et al.](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_fd4103a9-7e0a-4a81-a133-d1dde52f3c11)). A six-hour scan cadence therefore spends a large share of the useful window, and sub-hourly checks limited to high-score lookalikes are the budget-conscious response.

New tradecraft moves detection from the domain to the page. ESET's fake-CAPTCHA detection rose about 517% in the first half of 2025, and Microsoft attributed 47% of the initial-access cases its Defender Experts saw to ClickFix ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2025/06/26/clickfix-attacks-fakecaptcha-eset-report/), [RH-ISAC](https://rhisac.org/?p=17828)). Proofpoint reports tens of thousands of phishing URLs a month on the Lovable AI site builder ([Proofpoint](https://www.proofpoint.com/us/blog/threat-insight/cybercriminals-abuse-ai-website-creation-app-phishing)). In both cases the hostname is a compromised site or a trusted platform, so certificate and registration feeds see nothing. Detection needs content rules: clipboard writes beside "press Win+R" instructions, and credential forms on builder subdomains. Device-code phishing has no attacker-hosted page at all, so it belongs in customer guidance ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2025/02/13/storm-2372-conducts-device-code-phishing-campaign)). Signatures for adversary-in-the-middle kits decay within months: Sneaky 2FA randomised its distinctive 150-character URL path in April 2025 ([Push Security](https://pushsecurity.com/threat-intelligence/sneaky-2fa)). Every URL-shape rule should carry a last-matched date.

## Most "free" intelligence is closed to a paid product

The 2025–2026 trend is restriction. Maltrail moved its trail data under a new licence that requires separate written permission for any product, service or feed ([trails licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/stamparm/trails/main/LICENSE.md)). URLAbuse gated its downloads behind registration ([URLAbuse](https://urlabuse.com/public/data/phishing_url.txt)). Free web search has collapsed: Bing's API was retired on 11 August 2025, Brave removed its free tier in February 2026, and Google's Custom Search API closes on 1 January 2027 ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/lifecycle/announcements/bing-search-api-retirement), [Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/brave-drops-free-search-api-tier-puts-all-developers-on-metered-billing.md), [Brave](https://brave.com/learn/google-api-shutdown/)). There is no longer a free official way to detect search-result poisoning. The cheapest paid route, Brave at $5 per 1,000 queries, works out to about $285 a month for one query a day across 1,900 brands.

The platform's own NRD archive and DNS resolutions are the dependable history.

The ad libraries are free but narrow. They exist for EU Digital Services Act compliance, so Microsoft's covers only ads served in the European Economic Area since June 2023 ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/advertising/guides/ad-library-api)). Meta's returns commercial ads only where they reached the EU or UK ([adlibrary.com](https://adlibrary.com/posts/eu-dsa-ad-repositories-developers)). A campaign aimed only at North America will not appear in either. That is a real limit for a Canadian-incorporated vendor selling globally.

## What is verified and what is still a lead

Confidence varies by topic, and the ranking above reflects it. The phishing and domain feed facts are the firmest: each endpoint was fetched on 2026-11-13 with status, size and timestamps recorded. GitHub commit dates could not be read, so freshness is unverified for lists with no internal timestamp, including the MetaMask, polkadot-js and Phantom lists. Infrastructure and actor-dataset licence statements were read on the providers' own pages. ## Conclusion

The research inverts the question it was asked. A platform with about 50 feeds does not have a signal-supply problem. It has a signal-integrity problem (two reputation checks that cannot say "listed") and an extraction problem (kit, operator and mail-infrastructure evidence sitting unused in captures and DNS answers). The seven integration fixes and the Tier A items are mostly small, and several reduce legal exposure while adding signal. The owner's decisions are few: whether to pay Spamhaus or drop its query services, whether to seek written permission from SANS and ViriBack, and whether hosted DMARC reporting justifies asking customers to change a DNS record.

Free data is also becoming less free each year, so every adopted source should have its licence text saved at integration time and re-checked on a schedule. The durable advantage lies in signal that no licence can withdraw: the platform's own captures, its own NRD archive, and reports that customers' mail receivers send to it directly.
